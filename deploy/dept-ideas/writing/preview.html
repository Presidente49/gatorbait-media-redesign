Swagger, a Toll Booth and 28 Points Nobody Collected

Pull up a chair. Every week during football season this porch will hold the whole GatorBait staff's work in one sitting: what we saw, what we counted, what we argued about and where to go read the rest. One column, all the receipts, no reruns.

This week the porch looks out on a scoreboard that reads Florida 52, Ole Miss 28.

The night The Swamp got loud again

The numbers first, because they earned it. Florida beat No. 4 Ole Miss 52-28 at Ben Hill Griffin Stadium on Sept. 26 and did the heavy lifting late, outscoring the Rebels 21-7 in the fourth quarter (ESPN). The Gators are 4-0 and 2-0 in the SEC and ranked No. 8 (ESPN), the program's first 4-0 start since 2019. The voters noticed: Florida jumped 13 spots in the AP poll and 14 in the Coaches Poll and sits No. 8 in both.

I wrote Saturday night that The Swamp got its swagger back and welcomed the folks from The Grove in rude fashion. I stand by it. I am also going to let the rest of this staff talk me down a notch, because they did the work.

What the box score will not tell you

Brenden Martin did the arithmetic on how the 52 arrived: 31 of them came after defensive stops and big returns, including Jayden Woods' strip-sack, an early fourth-down stop and London Montgomery's kick returns. Eddie Gilley boiled the whole night down to five plays, from a blown Ole Miss fourth-down gamble to DJ Coleman's game-sealing interception. Chris Spears had the sideline and came back with 10 thoughts and two galleries of proof (one, two).

The honest ledger

Franz Beard has never let a win go unaudited. He counted at least 28 points the Gators left on the field: an overthrown Vernell Brown III, an open Amir Jackson on fourth down, a 67-yard touchdown erased by a hold and eight penalties for 92 yards. Loren Meadows heard Jon Sumrall say a version of the same thing in coach-speak: this team is still a work in progress. Both are right. You can beat the No. 4 team in the country by 24 and still owe yourself 28.

Who are these guys?

Franz borrowed that line from Butch Cassidy and pointed it at Phil Trautwein's offensive line, measuring what Athlon and Phil Steele thought of the group in the preseason against what it did to Ole Miss while Jadan Baugh scored again. The league weighed in Monday: Baugh earned his first SEC Offensive Player of the Week award, and Montgomery and Bryce Lovett collected their first SEC weekly honors. Franz's Tuesday thoughts opened with a picture of Trautwein and his family celebrating the win. That is the porch's favorite kind of photo.

The toll booth

Here is where the coach with the raspy voice took the poll out of everyone's hands. Sumrall told Florida he does not want to hear another word about Ole Miss, and he is making his team pay the toll on "Bloody Tuesday." Fair enough. Florida has lost three of its last four trips to Columbia. Missouri is 3-1 and 0-1 in the league (ESPN) after letting a fourth-quarter lead get away at Mississippi State. The Tigers will be in a mood.

The medical news helped the mood in Gainesville. An MRI confirmed a Grade 1 PCL sprain, the mildest grade, in Brown's knee, and sources told GatorBait he may not miss any games. Sumrall had good news on him Monday.

Off the field, still on the record

Two stories the porch will keep an eye on. The Senate passed the Protect College Sports Act 77-22 Monday night, and it is not law yet. And Denzel Aberdeen won a temporary injunction allowing him to play for Florida in 2026-27, which matters a great deal to the basketball backcourt.

Porch light

Kickoff is 3:30 p.m. ET Saturday, Oct. 3, at Memorial Stadium in Columbia, on ABC (ESPN). ESPN's FPI now has Florida favored in every remaining game except Texas and Georgia, and that math comes out to 10-2. I did that math myself Sunday and enjoyed every minute of it. Then I listened to Sumrall, and I put the poll down. Columbia has taken three of the last four from us. Pay the toll first. We will add it up on the porch next week.
