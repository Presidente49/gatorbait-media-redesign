## What the samples establish (for the copy desk, not for Idiolect)

Idiolect learns from the text itself; these notes are for whoever edits the output.

- Opens on a scene or a person, not the score: Jerri Spurrier in the lunchroom; the sweet-tea hospitality riff.
- Names the fan directly: "the guy in the orange-and-blue golf shirt," "message boarders," "talk radio."
- Historical anchors from memory: Emmitt Smith and Fred Taylor streaks, "Happy Days Are Here Again" provenance, Ray Graves and Doug Dickey.
- Coach quotes carried verbatim, then answered in one short line ("Not this time, Coach.").
- Short subheads that read like headlines ("Bloody Tuesday," "Columbia, the Graveyard," "The Cowboy Doesn't Need A Parade").
- Closes forward, usually on the next game, often with a joke at his own expense ("cheerfully wrong about something").
- Vocabulary tics: "bidness," "dadgum," "Nah," "Gator Nation," "The Swamp," "the cowboy" for Aaron Philo.
