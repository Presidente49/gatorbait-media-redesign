# Subscriber Win-Back Playbook

## Objective

Recover former paying GatorBait customers with useful, measurable reasons to return instead of blasting the entire old list with one generic discount.

## Controller scope

The controller may automatically:

- identify eligible former-subscriber cohorts from connected first-party data;
- exclude unsubscribed/suppressed contacts and anyone without a lawful contact basis;
- prepare audience counts and campaign drafts;
- select current articles/shows that demonstrate renewed value;
- create control/test cohorts;
- measure visits, starts, conversions and 30/60/90-day retention.

Owner approval remains required for changing price, benefits, trial terms or issuing a discount that is not already an approved offer.

## Cohorts

Keep these separate because their motivation is different:

1. **0–30 days since cancellation:** highest-priority human win-back window.
2. **31–180 days:** remind them what has materially changed and what they missed.
3. **181+ days:** treat more like a reintroduction to the modern GatorBait product.
4. **Failed-payment / involuntary churn:** service recovery, not a marketing pitch.
5. **High-value former members:** personalize around the shows, writers or coverage they previously used when data supports that inference.

Never mix failed-payment recovery into a promotional-discount test.

## Offer and message test

Start with a controlled A/B/C structure:

- **Control:** no win-back promotion beyond normal editorial/newsletter exposure.
- **Full-price return:** content-led reason to come back at the normal approved price.
- **Approved incentive:** if Brenden approves one, test a bounded incentive against full price rather than making the discount permanent.

A June 2026 Piano publisher test found a 20% discount branch converted at 0.71% vs. 0.38% for full price and generated more short-term revenue, while full-price returners showed deeper post-conversion engagement. Use that as a reason to test, not as a guarantee for GatorBait.

## Message sequence

### Touch 1 — What changed

Lead with product value, not guilt:

- current Florida coverage;
- named writers/shows they recognize;
- stronger digital-magazine experience;
- current game-week or season context;
- one clear return CTA.

### Touch 2 — Proof of value

Send only to eligible non-converters. Feature 2–3 genuinely strong current pieces or clips. The purpose is to demonstrate that GatorBait is active and useful now.

### Touch 3 — Last bounded invitation

Use the approved full-price or incentive variant. State price/renewal terms clearly. After this touch, suppress the cohort from the campaign for the defined cooling period.

## Frequency and annoyance controls

- one win-back sequence at a time;
- no repeated modal/email harassment after dismissal;
- no more than the configured campaign cap without a new owner-approved experiment;
- immediate suppression after unsubscribe;
- retain campaign/cohort IDs so the same person is not accidentally re-entered through another workflow.

## Website win-back

For identifiable lapsed members who revisit the site, test a restrained return message only after demonstrated intent, such as a second pageview or membership-route visit. Do not block article reading with an aggressive first-page modal.

## Measurement

Primary metrics:

- eligible audience;
- delivered/reached audience;
- membership-page visits;
- resubscription starts and completions;
- revenue per eligible former subscriber;
- 30/60/90-day retention by cohort and offer;
- unsubscribe/complaint rate.

Do not optimize only for immediate conversion. A higher-discount cohort that churns again quickly may be less valuable than a smaller full-price cohort.

## Recursive learning loop

`cohort → message/offer → send/onsite exposure → conversion → 30/60/90-day retention → compare → promote winning playbook`

A winning playbook may adjust timing, creative, audience segment and CTA. It may not self-authorize a new price, discount, benefit or consent rule.

## Research basis

- Piano Test Lab, June 24 2026: win-back test of control vs full-price vs 20% discount.
- INMA 2025 publisher benchmarks: past subscribers commonly represent a pool larger than the active subscriber base and systematic win-back is a major reader-revenue opportunity.
- Stripe subscription guidance: proactive engagement, churn feedback, failed-payment recovery and win-back campaigns are standard retention tools.
