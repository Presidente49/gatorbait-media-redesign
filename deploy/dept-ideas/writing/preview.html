The Buddy Martin Show, the morning after

We Were Right About the Wrong Things

The panel got the plot twist right. The details? Not so much.

On Tuesday, Sept. 16, Laura Rutledge, Ally Peek Wilbur, Carlton Reese and I gathered on The Buddy Martin Show to talk Florida football. Two games later, the Gators are 4-0, 2-0 in the SEC and ranked No. 8.

That’s a substantial change in the scenery. Florida won at Auburn 44-39 on Sept. 19, then beat No. 4 Ole Miss 52-28 at Ben Hill Griffin Stadium on Sept. 26, outscoring the Rebels 21-7 in the fourth quarter.

We had a few things right. And, as it turns out, a few things backward.

The Line of Scrimmage

Laura, who got her start with us at Gator Country, looked at the Auburn game and went straight to the trenches.

"For me, I'm looking at the line of scrimmage."

"We know that this is a line of scrimmage league."

Carlton, our columnist and a former Gainesville Sun man, had his own forecast for what an average offensive line could mean for Jadan Baugh and Aaron Philo. His final word that night was: "if Florida's offensive line is just average, I believe" Baugh is "gonna kill people, and Aaron Philo's gonna be able to hit open receivers."

Well, the scoreboard at Auburn and against Ole Miss says Florida’s line was more than average. Baugh and Philo have had plenty of room to make Carlton look prescient.

Laura’s broader take on Jon Sumrall’s Florida was that "what we've seen from Coach Sumrall is what we've needed at Florida." And, about the offense: "We're finally seeing a Florida offense that makes you say, 'Wow.'"

That one has aged well, too. Not a bad couple of weeks for Laura’s scouting report.

Haunted House, Happy Ending

Before Auburn, Carlton reminded us that Nick Saban called Jordan-Hare a haunted house. I said, "We know it's a haunted house. We've been there. Ask John Reaves what he thinks about it."

We got out with a win, but only by five points. That’s close enough to make the ghost stories feel less like ancient folklore and more like a useful travel advisory.

Then there was Ole Miss, who had LSU the week before coming to Gainesville. Laura thought Florida could benefit from the Rebels’ situation either way.

"I actually think Florida may be in a really good spot against Ole Miss either way."

"There is going to be some sort of letdown even if they win."

"Florida could get them at the exact right time, win or lose."

Laura called that almost to the letter. Ole Miss did come in after a demanding game, and Florida made sure the timing mattered. The Gators won by 24 and outscored the Rebels 21-7 in the fourth quarter.

I also told the story from Sumrall about his wife having gone to Auburn: "I don't care what the last name of the person is. If they're cheering for Auburn, they're not in my family," and "my mortgages are paid by the Gators."

That family policy appears to have worked out just fine on Sept. 19.

The Number We Wouldn’t Touch

Our season predictions were more cautious than the standings.

Ally, a blogger and podcaster, mother of two sets of twins and a legacy Gator whose grandfather, father and husband all played for Florida, picked 8-4. She added, "I think there's a world where they could finish nine and three. I think that's a stretch."

Laura said 7-5 and explained, "I'm sandbagging on purpose, 'cause I hope I'm wrong."

I said I was stuck at seven and a half wins. I had tried to get it to eight and couldn’t.

When I asked everyone for the one upset Florida could pull off this year, all three of us said Oklahoma. Carlton said, "Oklahoma looks like a very winnable game now." So we switched to Ole Miss.

I said, "How about both of them?"

Two weeks into the season, Florida has beaten both Auburn and Ole Miss and is 4-0. We all bet the under on this team’s win total. That prediction is already having a rough September.

The show had more than picks and line play. Laura told the story of taking a 5 a.m. job at WRUF because she thought it was 5 p.m. Her first sports report was dead air after she forgot to turn the button on. On opening day of her first Rays pregame show, a producer told her to put her IFB in, and she Googled what an IFB was on a flip phone.

"if I can do it, anybody can do it."

Her advice to young people in the business: "say yes to everything," and "You lead with knowledge. You lead with research." She also said, "it takes patience to be kind."

Laura said, "I still consider myself part of the Florida media because I can never leave," and she is coming back for Gator Growl and homecoming. The whole show is on our YouTube and Facebook pages.

Now comes No. 25 Missouri, Saturday, Oct. 3, at 3:30 p.m. ET, at Memorial Stadium in Columbia, on ABC.

I’m not raising my number until we get out of Columbia. The last panel that felt sure about a road game was us, at Auburn, and we were only right by five points.
