# GatorBait Magazine — Weekly Web Issue: Editorial Structure

Prepared Oct. 1, 2026, for the Florida at No. 25 Missouri issue (Saturday, Oct. 3, 3:30 p.m. ET, ABC). Research-only; nothing on the live site was changed.

Sources are inline. Season facts are from ESPN and FloridaGators.com only. Where the two disagree, both numbers are shown.

---

## 1. The one-page blueprint (about 8 sections, phone-first)

The model is the classic three-part magazine: a short front of book that orients, a middle built around one cover story plus a few features and columns, and a back of book of recurring departments that ends on a signature column. Sports Illustrated's early structure put departments such as Scoreboard up front and columnists in the back ("From page 53 on, columnists and special departments populate the back of the magazine"; search result for SI's 1954 publisher memo, https://vault.si.com/vault/1954/08/23/memo-from-the-publisher; the page itself now returns 404). SI tables of contents from the 2000s and 2010s show the same spine: Leading Off at page 6, Scorecard, Faces in the Crowd, features in the middle, Point After on the last page (https://vault.si.com/vault/2005/05/23/the-lineup; https://vault.si.com/vault/2017/06/05/lineup). ESPN The Magazine's departments ran in a fixed order (Two Way, The Biz, The Post, Zoom, The Jump, Outtakes, Life of Reilly) with The Truth as a back-page editorial (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN_The_Magazine).

The historical Gator Bait workflow is worth keeping: the weekly was printed Monday, "the bulk of the magazine was sent to the printer every Friday, with the exception of a game recap story which would be dropped in as the lead article" Sunday, plus a weekly feature by a named columnist (David Stirt, https://www.stirtstache.com/home/the-birth-of-gator-bait-newspaper). For a web issue, that translates to: build Tuesday through Thursday, lock Friday, slot the recap after the game.

Target total: 3,500 to 4,500 words on the page, plus two galleries. A phone reader should get the whole issue in 12 to 15 minutes of scrolling, with the cover story and pregame as the two deep stops.

| # | Section | Type | Purpose (one line) | Target length |
|---|---------|------|--------------------|---------------|
| 1 | **Cover / Lead** (Buddy Martin) | Rotates | The one story that sets the issue's argument; Buddy is the editorial lead by Brenden's direction | 900 to 1,200 words + cover photo |
| 2 | **Front of Book: "The Week"** | Evergreen | Five to seven dated items (awards, watch lists, rulings, scheduling) in newest-first order; the Scorecard equivalent | 300 to 450 words (40 to 70 each) |
| 3 | **By the Numbers** | Evergreen | Eight to 10 verified stats that frame the game, each with a source line (draft-only per Master Control; stays a department slot, never an auto-publish) | 150 to 250 words |
| 4 | **Columns** (Franz Beard; Thoughts of the Day) | Rotates | Named voices with a point of view; one Franz column in full, Thoughts of the Day linked as a running strip | 700 to 900 words featured + link list |
| 5 | **Feature** (original, one per issue) | Rotates | The "nobody else has this" story; the nugget Stirt taught UF students to find (see Section 2) | 1,000 to 1,400 words |
| 6 | **Pregame: Scouting Report** | Rotates (format evergreen) | Opponent first look, matchups, injury/availability, how to watch, line; the Athletic-style preview card | 600 to 800 words + table |
| 7 | **Departments: Schedule and Results, Depth/Roster Notes, Injury Report** | Evergreen | Reference blocks a fan checks every week; updated, not rewritten | 200 to 350 words combined, mostly tables |
| 8 | **Best Shots** (Chris Spears) + **Back Page** | Evergreen slot, rotating content | Photo essay (Zoom/Leading Off equivalent) followed by a short closing column or mailbag (Point After equivalent) | 8 to 12 photos with captions; back page 350 to 500 words |

Evergreen departments that repeat every issue: 2, 3, 7, and the slot for 8 (photos and back page). Rotating editorial: 1, 4, 5, 6 (the pregame format repeats, the content does not).

Rules that keep it one magazine, not a feed:
- One canonical URL per article. The issue page links to the article; it does not re-host the text.
- One primary editorial home for Buddy's lead (the issue cover), with the remaining news lists newest-first.
- No duplicate alerts: an item in "The Week" is a 40-to-70-word brief with a link, not a second headline.

## 2. What makes each section good, with examples

### 1. Cover / Lead
- One argument, stated in the headline and paid off in the last paragraph. SI's cover feature was the issue's thesis; everything else supported it (SI lineup pages above).
- Written by the publication's lead voice, under a name. Gator Bait's print model paired a named weekly columnist (Jack Hairston) with staff game coverage from the first issues (Stirt, https://www.stirtstache.com/home/the-birth-of-gator-bait-newspaper).
- Carries the strongest photograph. ESPN The Magazine built Zoom around "one large 'image of the week'" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN_The_Magazine); the cover should get the same treatment.

### 2. Front of Book: "The Week"
- Short, dated, factual. SI's Scorecard items were cut to roughly 20 lines and readers "are just as pleased—or infuriated—by a one-paragraph Scorecard item as by major stories" (SI publisher memo, search result; https://vault.si.com/vault/1954/08/23/memo-from-the-publisher).
- Newest first, each item with its own link. On3's Gators Online runs this as "Quick Hits" from the weekly press conference (https://www.on3.com/teams/florida-gators/news/quick-hits-from-jon-sumralls-weekly-press-conference); 247Sports does it as "Everything Jon Sumrall said Monday" (https://247sports.com/college/florida/article/florida-football-gators-head-coach-jon-sumrall-transcript-quotes-missouri-tigers-road-game-sec-290593106/).
- Include the stuff fans will ask about even if it is not football (the Aberdeen injunction belongs here, not in the football pregame).

### 3. By the Numbers
- Every number traceable. ESPN's pregame page is the model: ranks next to raw numbers (Florida 532.8 yards per game, 5th in FBS; 53.5 points, 2nd; penalty yards 89.3, 134th) (https://www.espn.com/college-football/preview/_/gameId/401856708).
- Pair a Florida number with the opponent's number so each line is a matchup, not trivia (ESPN: Florida time of possession 34:04, 7th; Missouri 29:53, 77th; same URL).
- Keep it to one screen. SI's "Faces in the Crowd" worked because it was scannable in seconds (https://vault.si.com/vault/2017/06/05/lineup).

### 4. Columns
- The byline is the brand. SI's Point After rotated "different writers" but was always a named, first-person column on the last page (search result, https://vault.si.com/vault/2007/12/10/the-point-after); ESPN's Life of Reilly and The Truth were the same idea (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN_The_Magazine).
- A column takes a position the news cannot. The Athletic's recurring "Mandel's Final Thoughts" is a franchise because the reader knows whose judgment it is (https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/7634445/2026/09/27/oregon-usc-florida-playoff-college-football-thoughts/).
- Running shorts (Thoughts of the Day) should be a strip of dated links, not full text, so they do not compete with the featured column.

### 5. Feature
- The nugget. Arnold Feliciano, who took David Stirt's UF sports reporting class in 1982 and later ran the Gainesville Sun sports desk: "He always taught me to have a little nugget in your story that nobody else would have ... have something different because when you cover a game, you're going to have a lot of the same things" (https://www.mainstreetdailynews.com/sports/ridaught-gator-bait-david-stirt-uf-ring-of-honor).
- Reported, not aggregated: a person, a place, a document. FloridaGators.com's Scott Carter built "The Max" around a grandfather's basement gym in Kansas City (https://floridagators.com/news/2026/9/25/football-the-max-effect-how-jayden-woods-was-built-Jayden-Woods-Florida-Gators-Jon-Sumrall). That is the bar.
- Check it against the week's mainstream run before assigning (see Section 4).

### 6. Pregame: Scouting Report
- Fixed card, then prose. ESPN's pregame leads with records, line, TV, then key stats, team leaders, last game and next game (https://www.espn.com/college-football/preview/_/gameId/401856708). FloridaGators.com's broadcast note adds crew, radio, replay times (https://floridagators.com/news/2026/9/29/football-broadcast-information-no-8-florida-vs-no-25-missouri).
- Matchup framing in both directions: "When Missouri has the ball / When Florida has the ball," with a measurable target for each (Rock M Nation, https://www.rockmnation.com/missouri-tigers-football/157622/mizzou-football-florida-gators-preview-homecoming-2026).
- Availability from the official report, with the probability language the SEC uses (questionable = 50 percent) (Florida Gators on SI, https://www.si.com/college/florida/football/initial-availability-for-brown-iii-stockton-singleton-jr-revealed-gators-matchup-vs-missouri-01m3t997184w; Gainesville Sun, https://www.gainesville.com/story/sports/college/football/2026/09/30/florida-football-wrs-listed-as-questionable-for-mizzou-vernell-brown-iii/91928485007/).

### 7. Departments: Schedule and Results, Depth/Roster Notes, Injury Report
- Team-site publications keep these as permanent navigation, not stories: Dawg Post's site menu is Schedule, SEC Standings, Roster, Staff, Depth Chart, Class Breakdown, Recruiting (https://dawgpost.com/georgia-football/depth-chart; https://dawgpost.com/football-recruiting). Blue & Gold Illustrated's print issue leads with "an updated depth chart" and "the latest football and basketball recruiting news" (https://www.magzter.com/ja/US/Coman-Publishing-Co./Blue-&-Gold-Illustrated/Men's-Interest).
- Schedule block is the official one, with results and kick windows exactly as listed (https://floridagators.com/sports/football/schedule/2026).
- Injury block cites the SEC availability report by day (Wednesday initial, Thursday and Friday updates about 8:10 p.m. ET, game-day about 2 p.m. ET) (Florida Gators on SI, URL above).

### 8. Best Shots + Back Page
- Photo essay first, with real captions (who, what, when). ESPN's Zoom and SI's Leading Off opened the book with full-bleed images; on the web, this is the best place for Chris Spears' galleries because it rewards the scroll (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN_The_Magazine; https://vault.si.com/vault/2005/05/23/the-lineup).
- Close on a voice. SI's Point After "is found on the back page and written by various writers" (search result; https://vault.si.com/vault/2007/12/10/the-point-after). ESPN's The Truth was "a back-page editorial" (Wikipedia, above). The back page can alternate: a short Buddy or Franz sign-off, a reader mailbag, or a "Pay the Toll" style staff note.
- Give it a standing name so readers look for it.

### Angle A: "Twenty-two throws." The quarterback nobody asks to carry the offense
- The fact: Philo has 87 attempts in four games (ESPN, https://www.espn.com/college-football/preview/_/gameId/401856708), under 22 a game, for 917 yards and a 72.4 completion percentage. ESPN lists 7 passing TDs and 2 INTs; FloridaGators.com lists nine TDs and two INTs plus 24 rushes for 69 yards and three rushing TDs (https://floridagators.com/news/2026/9/29/football-philo-showing-gators-who-he-is-aaron-philo-florida-gators-jon-sumrall). Resolve the TD count against the official box scores before publishing.
- The contrast: Florida rushes for 260 yards a game (11th) and Baugh has 600 yards on 83 carries with 11 TDs (ESPN, same page). Florida leads the country at 0.61 EPA per pass play (SI/FanNation, above). Against Ole Miss he threw 23 times (ESPN).
- Why it is untaken: the week's Philo coverage is about leadership and intangibles; the SI piece is about the whole offense. No outlet has written the volume question: what does a Faulkner offense look like when the quarterback is the least-used weapon and the most efficient one, and what happens the first time Florida has to throw 35 times from behind. Reporting path: Faulkner's Tuesday availability, the Georgia Tech attempt history (Philo "started just one game in two seasons" there, FloridaGators.com), Missouri's run defense (100.5 yards per game, 32nd; ESPN).

### Angle B: "The flag problem." Florida is 4-0 and 134th in penalty yards
- The fact: Florida averages 89.3 penalty yards a game, 134th in FBS; Missouri is 117th at 73 (ESPN pregame, https://www.espn.com/college-football/preview/_/gameId/401856708). Both teams are plus-4 in turnover margin (same page).
- The tension: against Ole Miss the offense "committed one procedural penalty — a delay of game for which Faulkner took the blame" (Orlando Sentinel/Sun Sentinel, https://www.sun-sentinel.com/2026/09/30/florida-gators-football-jon-sumrall-sec-missouri-tigers-jadan-baugh-aaron-philo-dallas-wilson-buster-faulkner/), and the Sun flagged Jamari Lyons' unsportsmanlike conduct as a defensive wart (https://www.gainesville.com/story/sports/college/sec/2026/09/26/florida-football-defense-special-teams-upset-ole-miss-jon-sumrall-jayden-woods-aaron-chiles-gators/91932804007/). Sumrall: "I see the warts" (FloridaGators.com, https://floridagators.com/news/2026/9/26/football-gators-saturday-was-real-gators-give-the-swamp-a-day-to-remember).
- Why it is untaken: ESPN's stat page notes it in one sentence; no outlet has broken down whose flags they are (offense vs. defense vs. special teams), when they come, and whether a 4.5-point road favorite can keep giving away 89 yards. A Missouri game decided by a fourth-and-17 in 2023 (FloridaGators.com, Sept. 30 story) is the natural frame.

### Angle C: "Fast points, slow clock." The Faulkner paradox
- The fact: Florida scores 53.5 points a game (2nd) and still holds the ball 34:04 a game, 7th in FBS; Missouri is 77th at 29:53 (ESPN pregame, URL above). Florida gains 76.3 percent of available yards on its drives, best in the country (SI/FanNation, URL above).
- The context: the Gators have scored 44 or more in every game (66, 59, 44, 52) for the first time since 2001 under Spurrier (FloridaGators.com, Sept. 26, URL above), and Faulkner's offense scored on five straight possessions against Ole Miss (Sun Sentinel, URL above). Missouri's own preview wants Florida to run more because the pass game is where the explosives come from (Rock M Nation, URL above).
- Why it is untaken: explosive offenses are usually described as fast; nobody has written how this one scores like a tempo team while possessing the ball like a Wisconsin team, what that does to Brad White's defense (allowing 248.3 passing yards a game, 113th; ESPN) and why it matters against Austin Simmons (947 yards, 11 TDs, 0 INTs; ESPN). A drive-chart story: average drive length, plays, and time, pulled from ESPN box scores.

Fallback if none can be reported by Friday: Franz Beard's "Trautwein's troops" O-line piece already sits closest to an untaken angle (two sacks allowed per the Sun Sentinel; Lovett SEC Offensive Lineman of the Week per FloridaGators.com, Sept. 30). Promote it to the feature slot and move the Faulkner column under the cover.
